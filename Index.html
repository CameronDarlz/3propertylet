How tenant billing works — the "gross lease with service charge" model

Tenants don't pay their own utility bills. They pay a monthly rent figure which covers their office, plus a separate charge for heating, electricity, and water based on their floor area. HBC buys all utilities in bulk and recharges each tenant proportionally. This is why company turnover (£124k) is higher than pure rent (£101k) — the extra ~£23k is utility recharges. The risk is that energy prices can move faster than the fixed per-square-foot charge rates.

Utility quick wins — actions to take in the first 6 months
Month 1
Commission a commercial energy audit across all three sites. Get meter readings, understand existing contract end dates. Use a free energy broker (Utility Bidder, Inenco or similar) to benchmark current tariffs. Hanover's gas bill in particular needs explaining.
Month 1–2
Get Hanover House's boiler inspected by a Gas Safe commercial engineer. A gas bill of £13,808/year for a building of this size is abnormal. A full boiler service, TRV fitting, and heating controls upgrade could save £2,000–£5,000/year alone.
Month 2–3
Install smart meters at all three sites if not already in place. This gives real-time visibility of usage, makes it easier to identify waste (overnight usage, always-on equipment), and is free through most energy suppliers.
Month 3–4
LED lighting retrofit. Walk each building and identify all non-LED fittings. Common areas (corridors, stairwells, toilets) with PIR-sensor LEDs pay back fastest. Apply for Salix Finance or GGBS funding — available for commercial landlords in Wales for energy efficiency works.
Month 4–5
Set up a PPM schedule. Find a local facilities management contractor or use a simple annual schedule. At minimum: boiler service (Q4), gutters cleared (spring + autumn), fire alarm test (Q2), electrical visual check (Q2), asbestos inspection (Q3).
Month 5–6
Revise utility recharge rates. Once you have accurate smart meter data and any new energy contracts locked in, recalculate the per-sq-ft utility charge rates and issue updated schedules to all tenants. Build this into every new lease as an annually reviewed figure.
